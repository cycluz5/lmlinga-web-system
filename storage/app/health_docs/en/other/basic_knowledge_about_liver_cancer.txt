Basic Knowledge About Liver Cancer

Key Points
Liver cancer occurs when abnormal cells in the liver grow uncontrollably. Although not all cases can be prevented, you can lower your risk by maintaining a healthy weight, staying physically active, getting vaccinated against hepatitis B, getting tested for hepatitis C, avoiding excessive alcohol consumption, and not smoking.

What Is Liver Cancer?
Liver cancer is a disease in which cancer cells develop and multiply in the liver. The liver is located in the upper right part of the abdomen near the stomach, intestines, gallbladder, and pancreas. Inside the liver is a network of small tubes called intrahepatic bile ducts that carry bile throughout the liver.
The liver performs many important functions, including:
Storing nutrients needed by the body
Removing waste products and old blood cells from the bloodstream
Filtering and processing substances from food, alcohol, and medicines
Producing bile, which helps digest fats and remove waste from the body

Signs and Symptoms
Liver cancer often does not cause noticeable symptoms during its early stages. As the disease progresses, a person may develop one or more of the following symptoms. These symptoms may also be caused by other illnesses, so it is important to consult a healthcare provider for proper evaluation.
Possible symptoms include:
Pain or discomfort in the upper right side of the abdomen
Swelling of the abdomen
A hard lump below the right rib cage
Pain near the right shoulder blade or upper back
Jaundice (yellowing of the skin and the whites of the eyes)
Easy bruising or bleeding
Unusual tiredness or weakness
Nausea and vomiting
Loss of appetite
Unexplained weight loss

Risk Factors
Several conditions and lifestyle habits can increase the risk of developing liver cancer, including:
Being overweight or obese
Long-term infection with hepatitis B or hepatitis C
Smoking cigarettes
Drinking excessive amounts of alcohol
Cirrhosis or scarring of the liver
Nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (fat buildup in the liver not caused by alcohol)
Diabetes
Hemochromatosis, a condition in which the body stores too much iron
Eating foods contaminated with aflatoxin, a harmful fungus that may grow on improperly stored grains and nuts

Reducing Your Risk
You can lower your risk of liver cancer by following these healthy habits:
Maintain a healthy body weight.
Exercise regularly and stay physically active.
Get vaccinated against hepatitis B.
Get tested for hepatitis C and seek medical treatment if needed.
Avoid smoking or quit if you currently smoke.
Limit or avoid excessive alcohol consumption.
