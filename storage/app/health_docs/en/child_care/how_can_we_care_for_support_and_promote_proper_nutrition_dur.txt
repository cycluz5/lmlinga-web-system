How Can We Care for, Support, and Promote Proper Nutrition During a Baby’s First 1,000 Days?
Pregnancy (270 Days)

Promote and support proper maternal nutrition before, during, and after pregnancy, including while breastfeeding.
Maintain a healthy weight before and during pregnancy. 
Eat a balanced and nutritious diet. 
Visit the health center for at least four (4) prenatal check-ups during pregnancy. 
Take iron and folic acid supplements daily to help prevent anemia. 
Use iodized salt. 
Learn the proper techniques for breastfeeding. 
Give birth in a health facility attended by trained health professionals. 
Baby’s First 6 Months (180 Days)
Promote, support, and ensure that exclusive breastfeeding is practiced during the baby's first six (6) months of life.
Immediately after birth, practice skin-to-skin contact by placing the baby on the mother's chest to keep the baby warm and promote bonding. 
Begin breastfeeding within the first hour after birth. 
Have your baby undergo newborn screening. 
Feed your baby only breast milk during the first six (6) months to support healthy growth and brain development. 
Do not give formula milk, water, or any other food or drink during this period. 
Visit the health center for the mother's and baby's postnatal check-up. 
Have your baby's weight and length measured every month to monitor growth and development. 
Baby from 6 Months to 2 Years (550 Days)
Starting at six (6) months, provide appropriate complementary foods while continuing breastfeeding until the child is two (2) years old or beyond.
Introduce age-appropriate complementary foods at six months while continuing breastfeeding. 
Feed your baby foods from at least four (4) of the seven food groups, including: 
Rice, corn, and potatoes 
Vitamin A-rich fruits and vegetables 
Meat, fish, poultry, and eggs 
Legumes such as string beans, hyacinth beans, and lima beans 
Feed your baby: 
2–3 meals per day at 6–8 months of age 
3–4 meals per day from 9 months to 2 years, with 1–2 healthy snacks such as fruits or bread. 
Bring your baby to the health center for regular check-ups, especially when sick, and for free Vitamin A supplementation and immunization. 
Talk to, play with, and show affection to your baby every day. A child who receives proper care, attention, love, and nutrition is more likely to grow up healthy, strong, and develop well.
