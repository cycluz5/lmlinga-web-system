MAIN SUBJECT: DIARRHEA

WHAT IS DIARRHEA?
Diarrhea is when you have loose, watery poop more often than normal. Almost everyone gets it from time to time. Most cases are mild. It usually passes within a few days.
Even though diarrhea is common, it can sometimes become serious.
Frequent watery diarrhea causes your body to lose water and electrolytes, which can lead to dehydration.
Dangerous for certains groups such as:
- Babies and young children.
- Adults age 65 and up.
- People who have a weakened immune system.
Most people can manage mild diarrhea at home. You need to stay hydrated and get plenty of rest. But you may need medical treatment if it lasts longer than expected or keeps coming back. It’s also important to see a healthcare provider if you have diarrhea along with symptoms like severe pain, fever or bloody poop.

TYPES OF DIARRHEA
Healthcare providers describe diarrhea based on how long it lasts:
- Acute diarrhea: Lasts a few days or less and usually goes away on its own.
- Persistent diarrhea: Lasts two to four weeks.
- Chronic diarrhea: Lasts longer than four weeks or keeps coming back.

SYMPSTOMS
- Stomach cramps.
- Stomachache or bloating.
- An urgent need to poop.
- Nausea.
- Vomiting.
- Fever.
Most diarrhea symptoms improve within a few days. But severe pain, bloody poop or signs of dehydration can mean you need medical care.

CAUSES
- Viral, bacterial or parasitic infections.
- Food poisoning.
- Medicines, especially antibiotics.
- Food intolerances, including lactose intolerance.
- Digestive conditions, like irritable bowel syndrome (IBS), Crohn’s disease and ulcerative colitis.
- Surgery involving your digestive tract.

RISKS FACTORS
Anyone can get diarrhea, but your risk is higher if you:
- Have close contact with someone who’s sick.
- Eat or drink contaminated food or water.
- Travel to areas with poor sanitation.
- Take antibiotics or certain other medicines.
- Have a digestive condition like IBS or inflammatory bowel disease (IBD).

TOO LOWER RISKS
- Washing your hands often with soap and water.
- Preparing and storing food safely.
- Drinking safe water, especially while traveling.
- Staying up-to-date on recommended vaccines, including the rotavirus vaccine for infants

MANAGEMENT

IF YOU HAVE DIARRHEA
The most important diarrhea treatment is replacing the fluids your body is losing. Drink plenty of water and fluids that contain electrolytes, like oral rehydration solutions or broth. If plain water upsets your stomach, try taking small sips throughout the day.
Eat small, frequent meals if you feel hungry. Foods to eat when you have diarrhea include bananas, rice, applesauce and toast. These bland foods are easier to tolerate while your digestive system recovers. It also helps to avoid foods and drinks that make symptoms worse. These can include caffeine, alcohol and greasy foods.

WHAT TO TAKE FOR DIARRHEA?
Diarrhea treatment depends on what’s causing it. Medications may include:
- Over-the-counter medicines, like loperamide or bismuth subsalicylate.
- Antibiotics or antiparasitic medicines for certain infections.
- Medicines to treat conditions like
IBD.
- Sometimes probiotics, if your provider recommends them.
Don’t take over-the-counter diarrhea medicine if you have bloody diarrhea or a high fever unless your provider tells you it’s safe.

FREQUENTLY ASKED

WHAT DOES IT MEAN IF I HAVE YELLOW DIARRHEA?
Poop comes in all different colors. If you have yellow diarrhea, you may have eaten something with a yellowish hue to it. But if it lasts more than a few days, talk to a healthcare provider. It could be a sign of a more serious condition.

HOW LONG DOES DIARRHEA LAST?
Most cases of acute diarrhea gets better within a few days. Persistent diarrhea lasts between two and four weeks. Chronic diarrhea lasts longer than four weeks.

WHAT IF MY BABY HAS DIARRHEA?
Babies and young children can become dehydrated much faster than adults. Contact your child’s pediatrician if your baby has severe diarrhea or signs of dehydration.

IS DIARRHEA CONTAGIOUS?
Sometimes. Diarrhea caused by viruses, bacteria or parasites can spread from person to person through contaminated food, water or surfaces. Washing your hands often and handling food safely can help reduce the spread.
Diarrhea caused by medications, food intolerances or chronic digestive conditions isn’t contagious.
