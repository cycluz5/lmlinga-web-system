PILLS
Birth control pills are one of the modern methods of family planning. They are taken by women every day to prevent pregnancy.
When a woman takes birth control pills, the ovary does not release a mature egg that could be fertilized by a man's sperm during sexual intercourse.
Combined Oral Contraceptives (COCs)
99.7% effective when used correctly. 
Contain “hormones” (estrogen and progestin), which help prevent pregnancy. 
Not suitable for breastfeeding mothers. 
Progestin-Only Pills (POPs)
99.5% effective when used correctly. 
Contain “hormones” progestin, which prevents ovulation. 
Suitable for breastfeeding mothers because they do not reduce the flow or amount of breast milk. 
Should be taken every day at the same time. 
Benefits of Birth Control Pills
Do not interfere with sexual activity. 
Help prevent anemia. 
Can be easily stopped if you decide to have another child. 
Help make menstrual periods regular. 
Reduce the risk of ovarian and uterine cancer. 
Progestin-Only Pills (POPs) are suitable for breastfeeding mothers. 
Possible Effects While Taking Birth Control Pills
Dizziness during the first three months of use. 
Spotting or light bleeding between menstrual periods. 
A feeling of heaviness or mild tenderness in the breasts. 
If you have any concerns, consult your healthcare provider immediately. 
Limitations of Birth Control Pills
You must remember to take the pills every day. 
You need to have an adequate supply of pills. 
The effectiveness of the pills may be reduced when taken together with certain medicines, such as Rifampicin (a medicine for tuberculosis) and medicines for epilepsy. 
Combined Oral Contraceptives (COCs) may reduce the amount and quality of a mother's breast milk. 
Birth control pills should not be taken by pregnant women. 
Birth control pills do not protect against sexually transmitted infections (STIs), including HIV and AIDS.
Things to Remember When Using Birth Control Pills
Take the pills every day. 
If you forget to take a pill, consult your healthcare provider immediately. 
Consult a doctor, nurse, or midwife at the nearest health center, clinic, or hospital to find out if this family planning method is suitable for you.
