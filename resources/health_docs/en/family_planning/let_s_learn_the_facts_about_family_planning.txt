LET'S LEARN THE FACTS ABOUT FAMILY PLANNING!

Family planning is the process of planning the desired NUMBER and the proper SPACING BETWEEN per pregnancies. This can best be achieved through the use of modern, safe, and effective contraceptive methods.

BENEFITS OF FAMILY PLANNING
Allows the mother to fully recover her strength after childbirth. 
Gives parents more quality time for their children, spouse, and themselves. 
Enables parents to provide their children with enough love, care, guidance, and proper values. 
Makes it easier to save money for children's education and other essential needs. 
Helps ensure children receive proper care, including breastfeeding, adequate nutrition, immunization, proper feeding, and good dental care.


FACTS AND MYTHS ABOUT FAMILY PLANNING!
1. Do birth control pills cause weight gain?
No.There are different types of birth control pills, and some may suit your body better than others. Some can help maintain your current weight and may even provide additional benefits, such as improving your skin. 
Weight gain is often caused by other factors, such as a slower metabolism with age or an increased appetite. 
2. Should women take a break from using birth control pills after using them for a long time?
No.Many studies show that long-term use of birth control pills offers several health benefits for women.

3. Do birth control pills accumulate in the uterus?
No.The hormones in birth control pills are processed and eliminated from the body daily through urine and bowel movements. This is why they must be taken every day to remain effective.

4. Do birth control pills affect breastfeeding mothers or their babies?
Breastfeeding mothers are recommended to use progestin-only pills, which do not affect the quantity or quality of breast milk and are safe for the baby.

5. Do birth control pills affect a woman's ability to become pregnant again?
According to experts, fertility usually returns within 1 to 6 months after stopping birth control pills.

6. Does menstrual blood build up in the body while using birth control pills or injectable contraceptives?
No. Menstrual blood does not accumulate in the body. Any changes in bleeding patterns while using pills or injectables are normal and do not mean that menstrual blood is being retained.

7. Do birth control pills cause pimples?
No. Some types of birth control pills can actually reduce acne and improve skin appearance.

8. Do birth control pills cause cancer?
No.In fact, birth control pills help reduce the risk of endometrial cancer and ovarian cancer.

9. Should you throw away an entire pack of pills if you miss more than one pill?
No. If you miss more than one pill, use a backup contraceptive method, such as condoms. Continue taking the remaining pills according to schedule. Read the product information leaflet for instructions on what to do if there is “Missed Pills”.

10. Do birth control pills cause headaches?
Each person's body may react differently to  Pills.
It is recommended to give your body up to three months to adjust to the pills.
If your body does not adjust within that period, consult a doctor about switching to a different type of pill and continue finding the one that works best for you.

11. Do birth control pills reduce sexual desire?
No. Give your body up to three months to adjust and determine whether the pill is suitable for you.

12. Are condoms effective as a contraceptive method?
Condoms are a highly effective method of preventing unintended pregnancies and sexually transmitted infections (STIs) when they are used correctly and consistently.

13. Are condoms durable?
Condoms are manufactured under strict quality-control standards to ensure their strength and reliability.

14. Can any oil-based household product be used as a lubricant with condoms?
No. Most condoms are made from latex. Latex condoms can be damaged or break more easily when used with oil-based products, such as baby oil, cooking oil, mineral oil, petroleum jelly, lotions, cold creams, butter, and margarine.

15. Can viruses such as HIV pass through condoms?
No. Research has shown that intact condoms provide an effective barrier against sperm, viruses, and other microorganisms.

16. Is a vasectomy the same as castration?
No. Vasectomy and castration are not the same. A vasectomy is a procedure in which the vas deferens (the tubes that carry sperm) are cut or sealed, while castration is a procedure performed on animals in which the testicles are removed.
A VASECTOMY DOES NOT INVOLVE THE REMOVAL OF A MAN'S TESTICLES.
17. What happens to sperm that is not ejaculated? Does it build up and cause the scrotum to burst or create other health problems?
No. Unused sperm is naturally broken down and absorbed by the body. It does not accumulate in the scrotum or cause it to burst or lead to any physical harm.

18. Can a man continue doing normal physical activities after having a vasectomy?
Yes. A vasectomy does not affect a man's overall health or physical abilities. After a short recovery period of about 2 to 5 days, most men can return to their usual activities.

19. Does a vasectomy reduce a man's masculinity?
No. After a vasectomy, a man will continue to produce his male hormones.
