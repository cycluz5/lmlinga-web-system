Hypertension
High blood pressure (BP), also known as hypertension, is a condition in which the force of blood pushing against the walls of the arteries remains consistently high over a long period of time. This can damage the blood vessels and the heart. Persistent high blood pressure may lead to serious conditions such as heart attack and stroke.

What Causes Hypertension?
There are two types of hypertension:
Primary or Essential Hypertension
This is the most common type of hypertension. The exact cause is unknown, and it usually develops gradually as a person gets older.

Secondary Hypertension
This type of hypertension is caused by another medical condition, such as kidney disease, thyroid disorders, adrenal gland disorders, or by taking certain medications such as birth control pills, pain relievers, and other medicines. It may also result from the use of illegal drugs.

Hypertension may also develop because of the following factors:

Aging
As people grow older, their risk of developing hypertension increases. It is more common among individuals who are 65 years old and above.

Overweight and Lack of Physical Activity
Being overweight or obese and not exercising regularly increase the risk of developing hypertension.

Smoking and Alcohol Consumption
Excessive smoking and heavy alcohol consumption significantly increase the risk of hypertension.

What Should You Do?
Take Your Maintenance Medications
Take your prescribed maintenance medications regularly as instructed by your doctor to help keep your blood pressure under control.
Exercise and Maintain a Healthy Weight
Exercise regularly to maintain a healthy body weight.
Aim for at least 150 minutes (2½ hours) of moderate physical activity each week. Examples include:
Brisk walking for 20–30 minutes daily
Cycling for 30 minutes daily
Zumba or aerobic exercise for 30 minutes daily

Follow a Healthy Diet
Limit Sodium (Salt) Intake
Consume less than 2,300 mg of sodium per day, or
Limit salt intake to less than 5,000 mg (about 1 teaspoon) per day.
Eat More Whole Grains
Choose healthy grains such as:
Whole wheat bread
Cereals
Rice
Pasta
Examples of one serving include:
1 slice of whole wheat bread
1 cup of cooked rice
1 cup of cereal

Blood Pressure Screening
For families with a history of hypertension, it is recommended that children and close relatives begin monitoring their blood pressure starting at 25 years of age to help detect hypertension early.
