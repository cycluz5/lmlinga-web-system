Basic Knowledge About Kidney Cancer

Key Points
Kidney cancer develops when abnormal cells in the kidney grow uncontrollably. Maintaining a healthy weight, avoiding smoking, and limiting exposure to harmful chemicals such as trichloroethylene can help reduce the risk of developing kidney and renal pelvis cancer.

Overview
Kidney and renal pelvis cancer is a disease that begins when cells in the kidney multiply uncontrollably and form a tumor. The most common type is called renal cell carcinoma, which starts in the kidney tissues.

What Are the Kidneys?
The body has two kidneys, located on either side of the spine behind the stomach and liver. Their main function is to filter waste and excess fluid from the blood to produce urine. The kidneys also help regulate blood pressure and support the production of red blood cells through hormone release.

What Is the Renal Pelvis?
The renal pelvis is the central part of each kidney. It collects urine produced by the kidneys and passes it into the ureters, which are tubes that carry urine to the bladder. The bladder stores urine until it is released from the body during urination.

Signs and Symptoms
A person with kidney or renal pelvis cancer may have one or more of the following symptoms, although some people may not experience any symptoms at all. These signs may also be caused by other medical conditions, so it is important to consult a healthcare provider for proper evaluation.
Possible symptoms include:
Blood in the urine
A lump or swelling in the kidney area or abdomen
Persistent pain in the lower back or side
Ongoing tiredness or fatigue
Frequent or recurring fever
Loss of appetite
Unexplained weight loss
Intestinal blockage
A general feeling of poor health or weakness

Risk Factors
Several factors can increase the likelihood of developing kidney cancer, including:
Being overweight or obesity
Smoking
High blood pressure
Long-term use of certain pain-relieving medicines
Certain inherited genetic conditions
Chronic hepatitis C infection
Kidney stones
Sickle cell trait, which is linked to a rare type of kidney cancer called renal medullary carcinoma
Exposure to trichloroethylene, a chemical commonly used to remove grease from metal surfaces in some workplaces

Reducing Your Risk
Although not all cases can be prevented, you can lower your risk of kidney cancer by following healthy lifestyle habits, including:
Maintain a healthy body weight.
Eat a balanced and nutritious diet.
Stay physically active.
Avoid smoking or quit if you currently smoke.
Follow proper safety measures if your work involves chemicals such as trichloroethylene
