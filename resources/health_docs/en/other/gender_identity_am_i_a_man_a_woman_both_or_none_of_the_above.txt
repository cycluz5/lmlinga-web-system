GENDER IDENTITY: Am I a Man, a Woman, Both, or None of the Above?

Confused? You're not alone. Many people are still confused about gender identity. To avoid misunderstanding, it's important for us to unlearn beliefs that may contribute to discrimination.

What are Sex Characteristics (SC)?
Sex characteristics are the external (physical) and internal traits related to a person's sex. These include chromosomes, gonads, sex hormones, genitals, and other physical characteristics that develop during puberty.

External or physical sex characteristics, such as the genitals, are usually what doctors or midwives use to determine a baby's sex during an ultrasound (and what many influencers reveal during their so-called#GenderRevealParty, which is more accurately called a #SexRevealParty) or immediately after birth.

What is Gender Identity (GI)?
Gender identity refers to a person's deeply held sense of their own gender and their personal experience of it. It may or may not correspond with the sex assigned to them at birth or their sex characteristics.

A cisgender person is someone whose gender identity aligns with the sex they were assigned at birth.

A transgender person is someone whose gender identity differs from the sex they were assigned at birth. For example, a transgender man (trans man) is someone who was assigned female at birth but identifies as a man. On the other side, if assigned male at birth, but identifies as a woman, they are called transgender woman (trans woman).
Remember: A person does not need to undergo gender-affirming surgery commonly referred to as "sex reassignment" or "sex-change surgery" to be considered transgender.

Some people do not identify exclusively as either male or female. They are often referred to as non-binary or NB. This includes individuals who are comfortable identifying as both male and female (or more than one gender), as well as those who do not identify with any gender at all.

WHY IS IT IMPORTANT TO UNDERSTAND GENDER IDENTITY?
Even at a young age, some people already recognize and understand their gender identity. Others may still be unsure about how to label or express it because of the expectations created by the traditional gender binary and gender stereotypes.

Many people can experience anxiety or intense stress due to pressure to conform to these expectations, even when they do not reflect their true gender identity. This stems from the reality that people who do not follow society's norms often face negative consequences such as discrimination, ridicule, harassment, bullying, and various forms of violence.These experiences can have long-term effects on a person's physical, emotional, and psychological well-being, as well as their livelihood and overall quality of life.

This is why everyone deserves support in expressing the gender identity they feel most comfortable with, along with the assurance that they are accepted and loved regardless of their gender identity.

So remember, however you choose to label or express your gender, that's perfectly okay—as long as it makes you happy and allows you to be true to yourself.

ALWAYS CHOOSE TO BE YOUR TRUE SELF!
Pretending to be someone you're not or hiding your true identity can be difficult, but expressing yourself may also feel challenging. If you feel ready, consider opening up to people you trust, such as close friends or family members.Remember that no one can force you—or stop you—from recognizing and sharing your true identity. It is your decision alone. Finding someone who will listen without judgment can be incredibly beneficial for your mental health.
Always remember that the way you identify yourself and choose to express your gender deserves respect from others, just as you should respect the identities and expressions of other people.
