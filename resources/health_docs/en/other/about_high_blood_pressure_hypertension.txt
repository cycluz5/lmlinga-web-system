About High Blood Pressure (Hypertension)

Important Things to Know
High blood pressure, also known as hypertension, occurs when blood pressure remains at or above 130/80 mm Hg. It often has no noticeable signs or symptoms, but if left untreated, it can damage important organs such as the heart, brain, kidneys, and eyes. People of all ages can take steps to maintain healthy blood pressure through healthy habits, and some individuals may also need medication to keep their blood pressure under control.

What Is High Blood Pressure?
Blood pressure is the force created when blood pushes against the walls of the arteries as the heart pumps blood throughout the body. A normal blood pressure reading is less than 120/80 mm Hg.

Hypertension develops when blood pressure remains consistently higher than normal, reaching 130/80 mm Hg or above. Because blood pressure naturally changes throughout the day depending on physical activity and other factors, healthcare providers diagnose hypertension only when blood pressure stays elevated over time.
The higher your blood pressure, the greater your risk of serious health problems, including heart disease, heart attack, and stroke. High blood pressure during pregnancy also requires careful medical attention because it may affect both the mother and the baby.

Diagnosis
High blood pressure is diagnosed by a healthcare professional through regular blood pressure measurements. The doctor evaluates both the systolic pressure (the top number) and diastolic pressure (the bottom number) and compares the results with established medical guidelines.
If you are diagnosed with hypertension, your healthcare provider will discuss your blood pressure readings and recommend an appropriate treatment plan. Monitoring your blood pressure regularly is important because it helps you understand your condition and track your progress.

Signs and Symptoms
High blood pressure is often called a "silent condition" because most people do not experience warning signs or symptoms. The only reliable way to know if you have high blood pressure is by checking your blood pressure regularly.

Causes and Risk Factors
High blood pressure usually develops gradually over time. Several lifestyle habits and medical conditions can increase the risk, including:

Lack of regular physical activity
Unhealthy eating habits
Obesity
Diabetes
Pregnancy
Long-term stress, anxiety, depression, or post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD)
Family history of high blood pressure
Environmental factor

While some risk factors cannot be changed, many can be managed through healthy lifestyle choices.

Health Problems Caused by High Blood Pressure
If left untreated, high blood pressure can damage blood vessels and reduce blood flow to vital organs, leading to serious health complications.

Heart Disease
High blood pressure makes the arteries less flexible, reducing the amount of blood and oxygen that reaches the heart. This increases the risk of:

Chest pain (angina)
Heart attack
Heart failure

Stroke and Brain Problems
Hypertension can damage or block the blood vessels that supply the brain, causing a stroke. A stroke may result in problems with speaking, movement, memory, or other body functions and can sometimes be fatal. Long-term high blood pressure is also associated with an increased risk of dementia and reduced brain function later in life.
Heart Valve Disease
Long-standing high blood pressure may damage one or more heart valves, affecting the heart's ability to pump blood effectively.

Kidney Disease
People with diabetes, high blood pressure, or both have a higher chance of developing chronic kidney disease because high blood pressure can damage the kidneys over time.

Prevention and Management
Regardless of age, everyone can take steps to maintain healthy blood pressure.
Healthy lifestyle habits include:

Being physically active for about 30 minutes a day, at least five days each week
Avoiding smoking
Eating a balanced diet that is low in salt and limiting alcohol intake
Maintaining a healthy body weight
Managing stress in healthy ways

Some people may also need blood pressure medications in addition to lifestyle changes. If you think you have high blood pressure or your blood pressure remains uncontrolled despite treatment, consult your healthcare provider immediately.

Keeping your blood pressure within a healthy range helps reduce the risk of heart disease, stroke, and other cardiovascular conditions.
