What is Stroke?
Stroke occurs when there is a sudden decrease or loss of blood flow into the brain. 

There are two types of stroke: Ischemic – caused by blockage of an artery supplying blood to the brain Hemorrhagic – happens when an artery bursts causing bleeding or hemorrhage

What are the symptoms?
The effects of stroke depend on what part of the brain is affected. Stroke are often sudden and require immediate treatment.

WEAKNESS OR NUMBNESS 
On one or many parts of the body including the face
SLURRY SPEECH 
Difficulty in speaking or incomprehensible speech
DIZZINESS OR DECREASED SENSORIUM 
Some experience dizziness Worst cases can lead to a coma
DIFFICULTY WALKING 
Some have difficulty walking due to loss of balance

What are the causes?
ELEVATED BLOOD PRESSURE
Uncontrolled blood pressure is one of the causes of stroke 
DIABETES 
Uncontrolled increase in blood sugar increases the risk for stroke 
HEART DISEASES 
Irregular heart rhythms (atrial fibrillation), diseases of heart valves, heart failure or a previous heart attack 
PREVIOUS STROKE 
A previous stroke can increase the risk of another stroke 
OBESITY, ELEVATED CHOLESTEROL
Being overweight or obese and elevated cholesterol levels increases risk of stroke 
PHYSICAL INACTIVITY 
Sedentary lifestyle increases risk for heart diseases 
SMOKING, ALCOHOL DRINKING, DRUG ABUSE 
Excessive alcohol drinking and smoking increases risk for stroke History or current illicit drug use can lead to stroke

What to do?
If you have symptoms of sudden weakness or numbness, headache or dizziness and slurry speech, you are advised to seek immediate consult by going to the ER

If you had a previous stroke, it is advised to continue your medications for blood pressure, diabetes and cholesterol control to reduce risk for having another stroke. Keep your blood pressure and sugar in check.

Exercises and avoid smoking and alcohol drinking 

Continue rehabilitation therapy for patients who had stroke to possibly restore some motor function for ambulation, coordination and speech
