Blood Donation Program

Blood donation is part of the Department of Health's National Blood Services Program under Republic Act No. 7719 (National Blood Services Act of 1994). Its goal is to ensure a safe and adequate supply of blood for patients in need.
Who Can Donate Blood?
You may donate blood if you:
Are 18–60 years old (16–17 years old with parental consent) 
Weigh at least 50 kg (110 lbs) 
Have a blood pressure of 90/60 to 180/100 mmHg 
Have a hemoglobin level of at least 125 g/L 
Have had 6–8 hours of sleep 
Have a pulse rate of 60–100 beats per minute 
Have a normal body temperature (35°C–37°C) 

Steps in Blood Donation
Registration 
Medical screening 
Donor interview 
Blood typing and hemoglobin testing 
Blood collection 
Rest and refreshments 

How Often Can You Donate?
Men: Every 3 months 
Women: Every 4 months 

Benefits of Blood Donation
Regular blood donation can:
Help save lives (one bag of blood can help up to three people). 
Reduce the risk of heart disease and heart attack. 
Help maintain liver health. 
Stimulate the production of new blood cells. 
Lower the risk of certain cancers. 
Help reduce cholesterol levels. 
Promote faster wound healing. 
Help maintain a healthy weight by burning about 650 calories per donation. 
Maintain healthy iron levels. 
Help keep the blood healthy. 

Who Cannot Donate Blood?
Temporary Deferral
Pregnant women 
People with fever 
Those who consumed alcohol within the last 12–24 hours 
Those who had a tattoo or ear piercing within the past year 
Those who recently underwent surgery 

Permanent Deferral
People with cancer or heart disease 
Severe lung disease 
Hepatitis B or Hepatitis C 
HIV infection or AIDS 
Individuals engaged in high-risk occupations (e.g., prostitution) 
Men who have sex with men (per current local blood donation policy) 
Unexplained weight loss within one month 

One-Year Deferral
Received an anti-rabies vaccine 
Received a blood transfusion 
Received a Hepatitis B vaccine 
Taking medication for syphilis or gonorrhea 
Women who recently gave birth 

Other Deferrals
Aspirin use (wait 3 days) 
German measles (rubella) vaccine (wait 1 month) 
History of malaria (wait 3 years) 
Mumps, typhoid fever, or measles (wait 2 weeks)
