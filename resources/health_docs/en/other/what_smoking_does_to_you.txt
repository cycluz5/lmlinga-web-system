WHAT SMOKING DOES TO YOU?

Components of tobacco smoke
Tobacco smoke contains:
Carbon monoxide – which reduces the oxygen carrying capacity of the blood, and is believed responsible for the reduced wind or shortness of breath, of smokers
Nicotine – has a temporary stimulant action followed by a depressing action on the nervous system. Repeated use builds up the smoker’s tolerance and he increases his consumption. It is a powerful, colorless poison. A drop of pure nicotine injected into the body would cause death in a few minutes. Heavy smokers who inhale while smoking absorbs enough nicotine each day to kill instantly if it were injected directly into the blood. People do not die after smoking because the nicotine absorbed at any one  time is less than a fatal dose
Carcinogen substance (coal tars) – cause changes in cells which often lead to cancer. Tar is a brown sticky substance also responsible for bronchitis, emphysema, and other diseases of the respiratory tract associated with smoking
Phenols – destroy protective action of the cilia (small hair like structure capable of sweeping movements) that line the respiratory tact
Ammonia – an irritant that cause a slowing action of the ciliated lining of the bronchial tubes
Arsenic – a carcinogenic as well as a poison which is found in trace amounts in tobacco

EFFECTS OF SMOKING
Cardiovascular system cigarette smoking
Double one’s risk for a heart attack
Increases heart attack risk with amount smoked
Increases heart rates 15-25 beats with one to two cigarettes
Constincts small arteries (arterioles) causing higher blood pressure
Increases chances of developing peripheral vascular disease
Causes carbon monoxide from smoke to rob oxygen carrying potential of blood
Causes increased free fatty acids in blood which may be related to heart attack

RESPIRATORY SYSTEM
Cigarette smoking
Increases risk of developing lung cancer tenfold for the average of one pack a day smoker
Increases lung cancer risk with amount smoked with length of time smoked, and with early age of starting
Is a major factor identified in the development of lung cancers
Is the most important cause of chronic bronchitis
Increases risk of dying or chronic bronchitis and emphysema about sixfold
Tends to paralyze bronchial cilia and stimulate over production of mucus eventually destroys ciliary structure and function, interfering with ciliary-mucous cleansing system, predisposing to respiratory infections
Causes increase in abnormal cell growth in bronchial tube walls with increase in nasal cell and thickening (pre-cancerous growth)
Causes closing of bronchioles, reducing effective breathing space and resulting in stretching and rupture of alveoli (emphysema)

CIGARETTE SMOKING IS ASSOCIATED WITH:
Five fold increase in cancer of the larynx
Four fold increase in cancer of the mouth
Three fold increase in cancer of the esophagus
Three fold increase in ulcer deaths
Double risk of bladder cancer
Double risk of death from cirrhosis
Fifty percent increase in cance of the kidneys
Greater incidence of infant prematurity and mortality 
Average smokers life expectancy is estimated to be reduced by 14 minutes per cigarette smoked

PASSIVE SMOKING:
Non smokers are exposed involuntarily during passive smoking to a composite of smoke generated in different ways during the burning of tobacco products. Such exposure is not only unpleasant but equally dangerous

Several epidemiological studies have reported an increased risk of lung cancer in non smoking spouses and children of smokers

Sidestream smoke is absorbed into lungs of by standers and raises the risk of the following (a)  bronchitis and pneumonia besides the more common effects such (b) as irritation of the eyes. (C) headaches, (d) nasal symptoms, (e) hoarseness and (f) palpitation

Passive smokers have higher rates of lung cancer than people who are not exposed (health alert, 1987). Sidestream smoke has nearly three times more of the carcinogens and noxious agents endangering non smokers.

Infants under the age of two are admitted more frequently to hospitals suffering from respiratory illness and slightly power development in lung functions

Other complications of smoking during pregnancy are higher risk of spontaneous abortion, placenta previa, abruption placenta, bleeding and premature rupture membranes, all of which contribute to increased mortality before birth
