GUIDE TO PROPER COUGHING TO PREVENT TUBERCULOSIS

What is TB?
• Tuberculosis (TB) is a contagious disease caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis. It commonly attacks the lungs and can be fatal if not treated early.

How does TB spread?
• The TB bacteria can remain suspended in the air, making it easy to infect anyone who inhales it. It spreads through coughing, sneezing, or spitting by a person with TB. The bacteria stay in the air longer when ventilation is poor or when places are overcrowded.

What are the symptoms of TB?
• A cough lasting more than two weeks, with or without the following:
Coughing up phlegm with traces of blood
Fever
Weight loss
Back and chest pain caused by other illnesses

Can TB be cured?
• Yes. TB can be cured through Directly Observed Treatment, Short-course (DOTS). In this treatment, the role of the treatment partner is important. TB can be easily cured if the correct medicines are taken for six months.

What should be remembered during DOTS treatment?
Take the medicine every day for six months under the guidance of a treatment partner.
TB becomes harder to treat if it returns.
Be diligent with treatment. Even if you feel better, do not stop taking the medicine before completing six months.
Have your sputum examined every two months to check if you are improving, and repeat the test after completing six months of treatment.

COUGH properly to prevent the spread of TB!
Use a handkerchief or tissue when coughing or sneezing, and cover your mouth and nose properly.
Mask or cover your nose and mouth if someone near you coughs or sneezes.
Use the proper place and method for spitting. Do not spit on the road or on the ground.
Be sure to wash your hands after coughing or sneezing, including between the fingers.
Okay to use your sleeve or the inside of your shirt if no tissue or handkerchief is available.
