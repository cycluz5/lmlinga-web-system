Clean Hands: About Handwashing

Important Things to Know
Proper handwashing is one of the simplest and most effective ways to protect yourself from illness. Many diseases spread because people do not wash their hands properly with soap and clean, running water. When soap and water are not available, using a hand sanitizer that contains at least 60% alcohol can help reduce the spread of germs.

Why Handwashing Is Important?
Regular handwashing helps prevent the spread of germs that cause respiratory infections and diarrheal diseases. Germs can easily spread from one person to another or from contaminated surfaces when you:
Touch your eyes, nose, or mouth with unwashed hands
Prepare or eat food without washing your hands
Touch objects or surfaces that contain germ
Cough, sneeze, or blow your nose into your hands and then touch other people or shared objects

Keeping your hands clean helps protect both you and the people around you from illness.

Important Times to Wash Your Hands
Washing your hands at the right times greatly reduces the risk of spreading germs. Always wash your hands:

Before, during, and after preparing food
Before and after eating
Before and after caring for someone who is sick with vomiting or diarrhea
Before and after treating a wound or cut
After using the toilet
After changing diapers or helping a child use the toilet
After coughing, sneezing, or blowing your nose
After touching animals, animal food, or animal waste
After handling pet food or pet treats
After touching garbage

Proper Handwashing Steps
Correct handwashing only takes a few moments but provides strong protection against germs. Follow these five simple steps:

1.Wet your hands with clean, running water, either warm or cold. Turn off the faucet and apply soap.
2.Rub your hands together to create a lather. Be sure to clean the backs of your hands, between your fingers, and under your fingernails.
3.Scrub your hands for at least 20 seconds. Singing the "Happy Birthday" song twice can help you keep track of the time.
4.Rinse your hands thoroughly under clean, running water.
5.Dry your hands completely using a clean towel or an air dryer.

Using Hand Sanitizer
Whenever possible, washing with soap and water remains the best method for removing germs. However, if soap and water are not available, use an alcohol-based hand sanitizer that contains at least 60% alcohol. Always check the product label to make sure it meets this requirement.
