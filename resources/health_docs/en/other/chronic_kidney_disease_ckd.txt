Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)
Is a condition in which the kidneys become damaged due to diseases such as hypertension, diabetes, infections, and other causes. In CKD, the kidneys can no longer effectively filter the blood, resulting in the build-up of excess fluid and waste products in the body.

What are the symptoms?
CKD often does not cause symptoms in its early stages because significant damage to the kidneys usually occurs before symptoms become noticeable.
Common symptoms may include:
• Swelling of the body (edema)
• Easy fatigability or feeling tired easily
• Dry skin
• Decreased urine output or absence of urination
• Muscle cramps
• Pale skin or shortness of breath

What are the causes?
Diabetes
Uncontrolled blood sugar is one of the leading causes of kidney damage.

High Blood Pressure
High blood pressure is also a major cause of kidney damage. Elevated blood pressure can damage the blood vessels that supply the kidneys, eventually leading to kidney failure.

Other Causes
• Hereditary kidney diseases
• Excessive use of pain relievers (NSAIDs) such as Mefenamic Acid, Ibuprofen, Naproxen, Diclofenac, and similar medications
• Autoimmune diseases such as Lupus
• Infections

What should you do?
Control Your Blood Pressure
Keep your blood pressure within the recommended range to help prevent further kidney damage. Take your maintenance medications as prescribed.

Control Your Blood Sugar
Maintain healthy blood sugar levels through regular monitoring, taking prescribed medications, and following a diabetic diet.

Consult Your Doctor or Healthcare Worker for Monitoring
Regular monitoring is important to assess kidney function. Certain laboratory tests may be required, including serum creatinine, which is used to calculate your Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR). The GFR helps determine how well your kidneys are functioning.

Make Lifestyle Changes
• Exercise regularly.
• Avoid becoming overweight or obese.
• Avoid smoking.
• Avoid excessive alcohol consumption.
• Get enough sleep (7–8 hours each day).

Diet
• Reduce salty foods and limit salt intake to no more than 5 grams or 1 teaspoon per day. This includes fast foods, instant noodles, canned goods, and processed meats.
• Eat the appropriate amount and type of protein, such as 85 grams per serving of skinless chicken, fish, pork, or beef.
• Choose heart-healthy foods. Reduce fried foods and fatty foods, and limit saturated fats and trans fats such as butter and margarine.
• As kidney function worsens, the intake of certain foods such as meat, fruits, and vegetables may need to be limited because damaged kidneys may no longer remove some substances effectively from the body.
