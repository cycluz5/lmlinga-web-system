A BEAUTIFUL SMILE BEGINS WITH HEALTHY TEETH
Oral, Dental, and Gum Health During Pregnancy
YOUR CHILD'S ORAL HEALTH CARE STARTS WITH A HEALTHY PREGNANCY

PLAQUE BUILDUP
It is a common misconception that a woman loses one tooth with every pregnancy. This is not true. However, pregnancy can cause changes in the health of your mouth, teeth, and gums.

One of the major changes during pregnancy is the increase in hormones, particularly estrogen and progesterone. These hormonal changes can promote the buildup of dental plaque, a sticky film of bacteria that forms on the teeth.

If plaque is not removed, it can lead to gingivitis, which is characterized by redness, swelling, tenderness, and bleeding of the gums. Plaque may also contribute to the development of pregnancy tumors (pyogenic granulomas) or lumps on the gums.

Pregnancy gingivitis commonly begins during the second trimester. If you already had gingivitis before becoming pregnant, the condition may worsen during pregnancy. If left untreated, it may progress to periodontal disease, a more severe form of gum disease.

PREVENT GINGIVITIS DURING PREGNANCY
You can help prevent gingivitis and other oral health problems by maintaining good oral hygiene.

Brush your teeth thoroughly, especially along the gum line.

Brush your teeth twice a day using a fluoride toothpaste, preferably after meals whenever possible.

Use dental floss once a day.

If brushing triggers morning sickness or nausea, rinse your mouth thoroughly with water or use an anti-plaque fluoride mouthwash.

Eat a nutritious diet rich in calcium (milk, cheese, and seafood), vitamin C (fresh fruits and vegetables), and vitamin B12 (meat and nuts) to help strengthen your teeth and gums


VISIT YOUR DENTIST
If you are planning to become pregnant or think you may already be pregnant, consult your dentist as soon as possible or schedule a dental check-up during your first trimester. This allows your teeth to be professionally cleaned and your dentist to assess the overall health of your mouth, teeth, and gums. Your dentist will also advise you on when you should return for follow-up dental visits during your pregnancy.

It is also recommended to visit your dentist during the second trimester of your pregnancy for a professional dental cleaning and to monitor the health of your mouth, teeth, and gums.
An additional dental visit may be needed during the third trimester, depending on the pregnant woman's condition. However, this consultation should be kept as brief as possible.
Non-emergency dental procedures may be performed during pregnancy. However, the safest and most appropriate time to undergo most dental treatments is between the fourth and sixth months of pregnancy.
If you experience a dental emergency, such as severe tooth or gum pain, treatment may be performed immediately. However, your dentist should first consult your obstetrician, especially if local anesthesia or medication will be required.

TOOTHBRUSHING TECHNIQUE
1.Brush the outer surfaces of the upper teeth by moving the toothbrush downward from the gums.
2.Brush the outer surfaces of the lower teeth by moving the toothbrush upward from the gums.
3.Brush the inner surfaces of the upper teeth by moving downward from the gums.
4.Brush the inner surfaces of the lower teeth by moving upward from the gums.
5.Brush the chewing surfaces of the upper molars using a gentle back-and-forth motion.
6.Brush the chewing surfaces of the lower molars using a gentle back-and-forth motion.
7.Brush your tongue thoroughly.
8.After brushing, use dental floss or a clean thread to clean between your teeth.

USE DENTAL FLOSS
Using dental floss once a day helps remove plaque from between the teeth. Be gentle when flossing to avoid injuring your gums. Follow either of the methods below:
The Finger Wrap Method:
Cut a piece of dental floss about 18 inches long. Wrap one end around the middle finger of your left hand and the other end around the middle finger of your right hand, leaving about 2 inches of floss stretched between your hands.
The Circle Method:
Cut a piece of dental floss about 12 inches long. Tie the two ends together to form a loop. If the loop is too large, wrap the floss around your fingers to make it smaller.
Once you have chosen the flossing method that works best for you, gently move the floss up and down along each tooth. Avoid pressing it too hard against the gums to prevent pain or injury.
