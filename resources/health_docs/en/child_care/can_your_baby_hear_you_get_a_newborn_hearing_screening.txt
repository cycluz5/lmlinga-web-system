Can Your Baby Hear You? Get a Newborn Hearing Screening!
What is Newborn Hearing Screening?
Newborn Hearing Screening is a test or examination of the hearing of newborn babies using Otoacoustic Emission (OAE). It is a law mandated by the government (RA 9709) requiring that all babies born in the Philippines undergo Newborn Hearing Screening.
Why does your baby need a hearing test?
According to the Philippine National Ear Institute (PNEI), 1 to 4 out of every 1,000 babies born have hearing impairment. 
Newborn Hearing Screening is an important test to detect hearing loss in infants so that they can receive prompt treatment. 
It is important to assess every baby's hearing from birth up to 3 months of age so that they can receive early intervention if they have hearing impairment. 
Can a baby pass the hearing test but still develop hearing problems while growing up?
Yes. This may happen because of accidents or trauma, excessive medication, or a family history of deafness.
If your baby does not pass the hearing test, make sure to have them examined by an ENT doctor as soon as possible.
What should I know about Newborn Hearing Screening?
Newborn Hearing Screening is safe and does not cause pain to the baby being tested. 
Before entering the testing center, the baby should be asleep during the examination to avoid inaccurate results. 
The results are available immediately before leaving the hospital. 
HEARING LANGUAGE AND MILESTONE
After Birth:
3 Months
Responds to loud sounds. 
Becomes quiet when hearing familiar voices and sounds. 
Looks at the face of the person speaking. 
3–6 Months
Turns toward the direction of sounds. 
Begins to laugh and make their own vocal sounds. 
6–9 Months
Begins saying "Mama," "Dada," and "Baba." 
Cries out to get attention. 
Turns when their name is called. 
Dances and sings when hearing music. 
9–10 Months
Imitates the sounds or words they hear. 
Understands simple words, such as ball, dog, and daddy. 
Turns toward sounds when they hear them. 
12–18 Months
Learns to understand new words. 
Follows simple commands, such as "Get the ball." 
Points to people, body parts, and toys when asked. 
Continues learning new words even if they are not yet clearly pronounced. 
18–24 Months
Listens to simple stories and songs. 
Combines words into two-word or longer phrases, such as "I will eat now."
